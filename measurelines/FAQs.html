Getting started

What does Measure Lines do?

It's a ruler for your phone's screen. You can float a transparent ruler over any other app, then drag, resize and turn it to measure what you see: a photo, a web page layout, a design mockup. Opening the app itself shows two rulers, one along the top and one down the left edge.

Which phones does it work on?

Any phone or tablet running Android 8.0 or newer.

Is it free? Are there ads?

It's free, with no ads and no purchases.

Why does it need “Display over other apps”, and how do I turn it on?

That permission is the only way Android lets an app draw a ruler on top of other apps. Android also insists you switch it on yourself in a system screen, so the app can't do it for you. You can withdraw it at any time in Android's settings.

  1. Tap Show ruler over apps (the icon on the ruler screen, or the switch in Settings).
  2. Tap Grant permission. Android opens a system screen.
  3. Turn on Allow display over other apps for Measure Lines, then go back.

If you installed from an APK file instead of Google Play, Android 13 and newer may say “Restricted setting”. Go to Settings > Apps > Measure Lines, tap the three-dot menu at the top right, choose Allow restricted settings, then try again. Installs from Google Play don't show this.

Why is there a notification while the ruler is on?

Android requires a notification whenever an app keeps something running in the background. Measure Lines uses it to keep the ruler running reliably, and it shows only the app's own status, “Ruler overlay is active”. Tap it to open the app, or use its Lock ruler and Hide ruler buttons. It never shows ads.

How do I turn the ruler on and off?

On: tap Show ruler over apps on the ruler screen, flip the switch at the top of Settings, or tap the Quick Settings tile.

Off: double-tap the ruler, tap Hide ruler on its notification, or use the same icon, switch or tile again.

How do I add the Quick Settings tile?
  1. Pull down your notification shade and open the tile editor (usually a pencil or edit icon).
  2. Drag the Ruler Overlay tile into your active tiles.

After that, one tap turns the ruler on or off from anywhere without opening the app. The tile and the in-app switch always stay in step. If the tile stops responding after the app hasn't been opened for a while, see the troubleshooting answer.

Using the ruler

How do I move, resize and rotate the ruler?
  • One finger, drag: moves the ruler.
  • Two fingers, pinch in or out: makes it shorter or longer. The markings update to stay accurate.
  • Two fingers, twist: turns it to any angle.

The first finger you put down is a fixed pivot: it stays pinned to the same spot on the ruler. The second finger drives the size and the angle. Touches outside the ruler go straight to the app underneath.

How long or short can the ruler be?

It can't be pinched shorter than 20% of your screen's width, or longer than the screen's longer side (plus a small overshoot). Its thickness starts at 48 dp and pinching can make it anywhere from 30 dp to 160 dp.

Why does the ruler start in the centre every time?

On purpose. Every time you turn it on, the ruler appears centred, unrotated, at a fixed size and unlocked, and it never remembers where you left it. That way it can't end up off-screen or shrunk to something you can't find or grab. Your settings (unit, colours, scale, calibration) are kept.

What is lock mode, and how do I unlock the ruler?

Locking keeps the ruler visible but sends every touch through it to the app below, so you can scroll, tap or draw underneath. While locked you can't drag, resize or hide the ruler by touch. If the ruler suddenly won't respond, it's probably locked.

  • Lock: tap Lock ruler on the notification, or switch on Lock ruler (touches pass through) in Settings (it's greyed out unless the ruler is showing).
  • Unlock: tap Unlock ruler on the notification, or switch it off in Settings.

Hide ruler on the notification still turns it off completely. The ruler always starts unlocked, so you can't get stuck with one you can't move.

What is the screen I see when I open the app?

Two fixed rulers, one along the top and one down the left edge. Touch either one and a cursor jumps to your finger and shows the reading. Tap a cursor to copy it. Thin guide lines from the cursors cross on the screen, and a pill shows the area of the rectangle they mark out (tap it to copy, or drag it to move both cursors).

The small arrow button at the right edge, just below the top ruler, opens the controls: units, show ruler over apps, set scale, crosshair, light or dark background, rotation lock and Settings.

Measuring accurately

Which units can I use?

Centimetres (cm), millimetres (mm), inches, pixels (px) and dp, the density-independent pixels Android layouts use. Change the unit from the Units menu on the ruler screen or in Settings, and a running ruler updates straight away.

cm, mm and inch are physical units and use your calibration. px and dp are measured straight from the screen, so calibration doesn't affect them.

How do I calibrate the ruler, and why would I?

Phones don't always report their screen size accurately (it varies by manufacturer), so out of the box the markings are close but may be slightly off. Calibrating once fixes that for cm, mm and inch.

  1. In Settings, tap Open calibration (Calibrate for accuracy).
  2. Hold a real ruler against the screen, or a bank, credit or ID card, which is exactly 8.56 cm (3.37 in) wide.
  3. Drag the slider until the on-screen bar matches your reference exactly.
  4. Tap Save calibration.

Reset to default on the same screen undoes it.

Can it measure real objects through the camera?

No. Measure Lines measures what's shown on your screen, not the room around you, and it doesn't use the camera. It does not read, capture or record the content of other apps or of your screen.

Measuring aids

How do the angle badge and 45° snapping work?

While you twist the ruler with two fingers, a small badge shows its angle in whole degrees (0° is horizontal, increasing clockwise) and stays for about a second and a half after you let go. With snapping on, the ruler clicks into 0°, 45°, 90°, 135° and so on when you're within about 4°, with a light vibration tick each time.

Both are on by default. To turn them off, open Settings > Measuring aids and switch off Show angle while rotating and/or Snap to 45° angles. A plain pinch to resize never snaps or nudges the angle.

What is the measurement cursor, and how do I copy a reading?

Switch on Measurement cursor in Settings > Measuring aids (it's off by default). A thin red line with a round handle appears across the floating ruler, and a small pill shows the reading from the ruler's zero end in the current unit, for example 3.42 cm.

  • Move it: drag near the line. It slides along the ruler only.
  • Copy it: tap the round handle.

The cursor starts mid-ruler each time the ruler is turned on. The two rulers on the launch screen always have cursors.

How do I measure a map or blueprint in real distance?

A custom scale multiplies every number the ruler shows, so the ruler reads in real-world distance. The tick marks stay where they are; only the numbers change.

If you know the map ratio (say 1:25000): set the unit to cm, open the Scale card in Settings, switch on Use a custom scale, tap the Custom… chip, enter 25000 and choose metres. It previews 1 cm = 250 m. After tapping OK, something that measures 4 cm on screen reads 1000 m.

If the map has a scale bar (say it's 2 cm long and labelled 5 km): on the ruler screen, touch a ruler so its cursor sits at the end of the bar (measured from zero), tap Set scale, and enter 5 with the label km. Everything now reads in kilometres. If you zoom the map afterwards, set the scale again.

The ratio calculator needs a physical unit (cm, mm or inch). With px or dp, use Set scale instead. A small caption such as ×50 m shows while a scale is on. Switch Use a custom scale off to go back to normal.

Colours & layout

Can I change the ruler's colour or make it more see-through?

Yes. In the Appearance card in Settings, pick one of six colour themes (Classic, Dark, Amber, High contrast, Blue or Green) and set the opacity from 30% to 100% (100% is the default). Opacity fades the ruler's body more than its markings, so the numbers stay readable. Changes show instantly on a running ruler.

The app's own Light, Dark or System look is a separate setting (App theme).

Does it work on tablets and in landscape?

It works on both phones and tablets. On the ruler screen, landscape is handy for longer lengths: the screen follows auto-rotate by default, and the rotation lock icon in the controls freezes the current orientation while you measure.

Permissions & privacy

Does it collect my data, need the internet or ask for lots of permissions?

It collects nothing and sends nothing. There's no account, no server, no ads, no analytics, and the app doesn't even have Android's internet permission, so it works fully offline and can't go online at all.

It asks only for Display over other apps (to draw the ruler), a foreground service (to keep it running) and notifications (Android requires one while it runs). It doesn't ask for storage, contacts, location, camera, microphone or accessibility access. See the privacy policy for the full detail.

What does it save on my phone?

A few settings, kept in the app's private storage and never sent anywhere: your unit, colour theme and opacity, app theme, whether the cursor, crosshair, angle badge and snapping are on, your custom scale, your calibration number, and whether the ruler is currently on or locked. The ruler's position, size and rotation are not saved.

The app opts out of Android's automatic backup, so these aren't copied to your Google account or a new phone. Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling deletes everything.

What does it do with my clipboard?

It writes to the clipboard only when you tap a cursor reading or the area pill, and what it copies is just that one measurement, such as “3.42 cm”. It never reads what's on your clipboard. While something is on the clipboard, other software on your phone, such as a keyboard or a clipboard-sync feature, may be able to see it.

Troubleshooting

The tile or ruler does nothing after I haven't opened the app for a while

On Vivo, Xiaomi, Oppo, OnePlus and Samsung phones, the maker's own battery management can stop a background app from starting anything once it has been closed, even from the Quick Settings tile. If the tile works right after you've had the app open but silently does nothing later, this is almost always the cause. It's a one-time setting you turn on yourself, because the app can't request it:

  • Vivo / iQOO: open the i Manager app > App manager > Autostart and enable Measure Lines. Also check Settings > Battery > Background power consumption management and allow Measure Lines.
  • Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO: Security app > App management > Permissions > Autostart and enable Measure Lines.
  • Oppo / OnePlus / Realme: Settings > Battery > App battery management (or Battery optimization), find Measure Lines and allow background activity or auto-launch.
  • Samsung: Settings > Apps > Measure Lines > Battery and choose Unrestricted. Also make sure it isn't listed under “Put unused apps to sleep” in Settings > Battery and device care > Background usage limits.

Menu names vary a little between software versions.

I can't see the ruler over some screens

Some apps and system screens hide overlays on purpose. Android's own Settings app is one example. That's Android's behaviour, not something Measure Lines can switch off.

Touches near the corners of a rotated ruler don't reach the app below

When the ruler is turned to an angle that isn't straight up or sideways, there's a very small area right at its corners where a touch can still land on the ruler instead of passing through. That comes from how Android handles touch regions for overlays in general. It shrinks to nothing as soon as the ruler is level again.
