Safety & privacy

What is a password manager?

An app that remembers your logins for you. You remember one master password; it keeps every other password locked away, makes strong ones for you and fills them in, so each account can have its own.

What is Native Creds?

A password manager for Android that keeps your passwords in an encrypted file on your phone. No account, no server of ours, no ads. It needs Android 8.0 or newer.

Why not just use Chrome's saved passwords or a notes app?

Chrome is far better than reusing one password. A dedicated manager adds a vault you can lock separately, works the same in every app and browser, and isn't tied to one company's account. Notes apps aren't encrypted and fill nothing in.

Is Native Creds safe?

Your passwords sit in one encrypted file that only your master password (and key file, if you use one) opens, and the app can't go online, so nothing can leave your phone. Having everything in one place is a fair trade-off if your master password is strong and you keep a backup.

Where are my passwords stored?

In a vault file on your phone, in the place you pick, such as phone storage or Google Drive. It's encrypted either way. Native Creds has no server, so no copy is kept anywhere you didn't choose. Uninstalling the app doesn't delete your vault file.

Does it need internet or an account?

No account, ever, and no internet. The app has no internet permission, so it can't go online even if it wanted to. If you want sync, your Drive app does the uploading, not Native Creds.

Who makes it, and can anyone see my passwords?

One independent developer (Manideep's Crafty Apps). I can't see your passwords: there's no server, no analytics and no crash reporting. There are no ads or trackers, and no subscription or in-app purchases in this version. In Drive, Google stores only the encrypted file.

Is it open source or independently audited?

Not at the moment: the code isn't published and there has been no independent security audit. What protects you instead: the vault is a standard, open file format, and with no internet permission Android itself stops the app sending data off your phone.

What if I forget my master password?

It can't be recovered, by anyone, including me: there's no back door. Keep a written copy somewhere safe. If your fingerprint or screen lock still opens the vault, copy your logins into a new vault with a new master password.

What if I lose my phone?

Whoever finds it still needs your master password to open the vault. Lock or wipe the phone with Google's find-your-device tools, then open your vault from Drive or your backup on a new phone. If the only copy was on the lost phone, it's gone, so keep a backup (how).

What permissions does it ask for?
  • Fingerprint / screen lock: to show your phone's own unlock prompt. Your phone handles the fingerprint; the app never sees it.
  • Short background service: only to clear the clipboard 30 seconds after you copy.
  • Nothing else: no internet, storage, contacts, location, camera or microphone.

Getting started

How do I set it up?
  1. Tap Create a new vault and choose a master password.
  2. Switch on fingerprint or screen lock if you'd like (optional).
  3. Pick where to save the file: phone storage, or Google Drive to use it on other devices.

Leave Use as my default vault on (why).

What's the best way to set it up?

Three steps, once. After that, signing in shows only your phone's unlock prompt.

  1. Make your vault the default (already on for your first vault).
  2. Turn on fingerprint or screen lock, when creating the vault or via Turn on quick unlock in its ⋮ menu. Needs a screen lock.
  3. Tap Set as default on the home screen so it fills logins (how).

You still tap Unlock, then your login. Keep your master password: a new phone needs it.

How strong should my master password be?

Long beats clever: five or six random words you can remember, used nowhere else. Avoid names, dates and song lyrics. A strength meter shows as you type it.

Do I need a key file?

No. A strong master password alone is fine. A key file is an extra file that works like a physical key: stronger, but lose it and you lose the vault. Keep it away from the vault file.

What's a default vault, and why set one?

It's the vault autofill opens and where new logins are saved. Without one, Native Creds has to ask which file to use every time. You can keep several vaults under My vaults and change the default in Settings.

Can I bring my passwords from Chrome or another manager?

Yes, from a CSV export: Google Password Manager, Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, Bitwarden, LastPass, Dashlane, NordPass and Proton Pass. You check duplicates before anything is added. Passkeys can't be imported, and 1Password's own file isn't supported yet.

A CSV holds passwords as plain text, so delete it afterwards. The app offers to.

How do I use it on two phones?
  1. On the first phone, create the vault in Google Drive (or move it there).
  2. On the second, install Native Creds, tap Open an existing vault, pick Drive and choose the same file.

Both phones need access to that file. Changes are combined automatically.

Everyday use

How do I turn on autofill?
  1. In Native Creds, tap Set as default on the home screen, then Choose Native Creds as autofill service.
  2. In Android's list, pick Native Creds. (Android 14+: Passwords, passkeys & accounts > Preferred service. Older: Settings > System > Languages & input > Autofill service.)
  3. On a login page, tap the username or password field, tap the Native Creds bar, unlock and pick your login.
Autofill isn't showing up. What should I check?
  • Settings should say Native Creds is your autofill service.
  • Chrome needs version 135 or newer.
  • The entry's website address must match the site.
  • Tap another field, then back; or touch and hold the field and choose Autofill.
  • Some apps and sites block autofill on purpose.

Still stuck? Settings > Autofill setup & diagnostics has an activity log.

Is autofill safe?

Generally safer than typing: it matches the app or site address, so a lookalike phishing page gets no suggestion, and nothing sits on the clipboard. Turn autofill on only for a service you trust, because Android shows it the sign-in screens you use. Native Creds always makes you unlock and tap first.

Do I have to open the app every time I sign in?

No. With a default vault and fingerprint or screen lock on, you tap your phone's Unlock bar, use your unlock, then tap your login. No Native Creds screen shows. If the unlock fails or there's no default vault, the app's own unlock screen appears instead.

Will it offer to save new logins?

Yes, once Native Creds is your autofill service. After you sign in somewhere new, Android asks whether to save it to Native Creds; nothing is saved unless you tap Save. A changed password updates the entry, and the old one stays in its history.

Can I unlock with my fingerprint or screen lock?

Yes. Switch it on for each vault when you create it, or later from its ⋮ menu (Turn on quick unlock). Your master password always works as a backup, and you can turn it off any time (how it works).

How does device unlock work, and how safe is it?

It stands in for your master password on that phone, so it's only as strong as your phone's lock: anyone who can unlock your phone can open the vault. Native Creds asks Android for a strong fingerprint or your screen lock (PIN, pattern or password).

  • Basic face unlock isn't accepted for this. A few phones with strong face unlock (some newer Pixels) may offer it.
  • On Android 10 and older, only a fingerprint works, not the screen lock.
  • If you add or remove a fingerprint or change your screen lock, type your master password once and turn it on again.
Can it make my 2FA codes?

Yes. On the site's 2FA setup page choose can't scan the code and copy the key. In the entry tap Set up two-factor code and paste it; the 6-digit code shows with a countdown. There's no QR scanner yet.

After autofill, the code is copied ready to paste (you can switch that off in Settings).

Is it OK to keep 2FA codes and passwords together?

It's convenient but a trade-off: whoever opens the vault gets both. For most accounts that's still far better than no second step. For your email or bank, some people prefer a separate authenticator app or security key. Your call.

Can it make strong passwords for me?

Yes. In an entry, use the generator next to the password field: choose a length (8 to 64) and which kinds of characters to include. Passwords are made on your phone.

Can I attach files to a login?

Yes: recovery codes, a scan, a key file. They're stored encrypted inside the vault. Files over 5 MB trigger a warning and over 20 MB are refused. Saving one out makes an ordinary, unprotected copy, so the app warns you first.

How do I organise logins, and can I undo a delete?

Make folders from a vault's ⋮ menu, search from the search box and sort from ⋮ > Sort by. Touch and hold to select several, then move, copy or delete. Deleting is permanent: there's no recycle bin or undo, so it asks first.

When does the vault lock itself?

The moment you leave the app: pressing home, switching apps or turning the screen off. There's no timer to set. Opening a web link from the app, like these help pages, counts as leaving too.

Why can't I take screenshots, and what happens to what I copy?

Screenshots, screen recording and the recent-apps preview are blocked on purpose, so a passing glance or a screen-sharing app can't capture your vault.

Copied passwords and codes clear after 30 seconds, even if you've switched apps. Anything else you copy in that time is cleared too.

Syncing & backup

The app has no internet permission. How can it sync?

It doesn't: your Drive app does. Native Creds reads and writes the vault file through Android's file picker, and Drive moves it to the cloud. Google Drive is the one tested on real phones; others like Dropbox should work the same way but aren't verified.

To fetch the newest copy at once, use Sync now in the ⋮ menu, or pull down on the list.

What if two phones change the same login?

Native Creds re-reads the file before saving and combines both versions. New logins from either phone are kept. If the same login was edited on both, the newer edit wins and the older one stays in that login's history. You'll see “Merged changes made on another device”.

How do I back up my vault or move to a new phone?

Your vault is one file, so a backup is a copy of it: use Save a copy… in its ⋮ menu, or copy the file anywhere (and any key file, somewhere else).

On a new phone, tap Open an existing vault, pick the file and enter your master password, then turn autofill and device unlock on again.

It says “Changes not saved to the vault file”. What now?

Native Creds refused to overwrite the file because something looked unsafe, for example it was only half-synced. Nothing was overwritten and your changes are still in the app. Keep it open and tap Try again; if it keeps failing, tap Save a copy…. Don't close the app first.

Compatibility

What does “KeePass file” mean?

KeePass is a long-established password manager for computers whose file format is public. Many apps read and write it (KeePassXC, KeePassDX, Strongbox, KeePassium), so you're never stuck with one app. Native Creds is independent and not affiliated with the KeePass project.

Can I open a vault I already have?

Yes. Native Creds opens KeePass files protected by a password, a key file or both. Tap Open an existing vault, or choose Native Creds from your file manager, Drive or an email attachment. Vaults from KeePassXC, Strongbox and KeePassium were tested both ways.

Can I open my passwords on a computer or iPhone?

Yes, with another KeePass app: KeePassXC on Windows, Mac or Linux, or Strongbox or KeePassium on iPhone. Keep the vault in Drive and open the same file there. Native Creds itself is Android only.

Limits & help

What can't it do yet?
  • Passkeys (planned, no date).
  • Scan a QR code for 2FA (paste the key instead).
  • Change a vault's master password inside the app (use KeePassXC, or copy to a new vault).
  • Edit custom fields made by other apps (view and copy only).
  • Import 1Password's own file, or check for breached passwords.
  • iPhone, Mac, Windows or browser versions: Android only for now.
Does it support passkeys?

Not yet. It can't create, store or use them, and passkeys aren't part of the CSV exports used for import. Passkeys saved in Google or on your phone keep working as before. Planned for after launch, with no date promised.
