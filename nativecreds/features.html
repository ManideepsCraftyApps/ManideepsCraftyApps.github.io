Android password manager

Your passwords, in a file you own.

Native Creds keeps your logins in one encrypted KeePass vault that lives wherever you put it: on your phone, or in your own cloud drive. No account to make, no server of ours, and no way for the app to reach the internet.

  • No account
  • No server
  • No internet permission
  • No ads or trackers

Read the privacy policy.

Nothing leaves your phone

Most password managers keep your vault on their servers. Native Creds does not have any. It does not even ask Android for permission to use the internet, so it has no way to send your passwords, or anything else, anywhere.

There is no sign-up and no sign-in. Create a vault, pick a master password, and you are in.

Because nobody else holds a copy, nobody can reset a forgotten master password. Keep a backup of your vault file.

A real KeePass vault, not a locked box

Your vault is a standard KeePass file (KDBX 3 or 4), so you are never locked in. Open the same file in KeePassXC, KeePassDX, Strongbox, KeePassium and other KeePass-compatible apps, and move on any time.

  • Master password, key file, or both protect each vault
  • Several vaults, with one set as your default
  • Open a vault from anywhere: a file manager, Drive or a mail attachment can hand a .kdbx file straight to Native Creds

Keep things sorted, find the one you want

Folders, search across the whole vault, and sorting your way. Select several entries at once to move, copy or delete them, or copy entries and whole folders from one vault to another.

Copy or reveal with one tap

Each entry keeps a title, username, password, website and notes. The password stays hidden until you ask to see it, and copying takes one tap.

Copied passwords are cleared from the clipboard after 30 seconds, even if you have already switched to the app you are pasting into.

Two-factor codes, in the same place

Store a two-factor (TOTP) key in an entry and Native Creds shows the current 6-digit code, counting down, so you do not need a separate authenticator app for it. Codes saved by KeePassXC, KeePassDX and KeePass 2 are recognised.

After autofill fills a login that has a code, the code can be copied for you to paste on the next page (you can switch that off in Settings).

Keeping the password and the code together is a convenience trade-off; the FAQs explain it. Scanning a QR code is not supported yet: paste the key instead.

Keep small files with an entry

Attach recovery codes, a key file or a scan to the entry it belongs to. Attachments are encrypted inside your vault file, and you can save one back out whenever you need it.

Saving an attachment out creates an ordinary, unencrypted copy wherever you put it; Native Creds asks first.

Fills logins in apps and browsers

Choose Native Creds as your Android autofill service and it offers your saved login right where you are signing in, in apps and in browsers such as Chrome and Firefox.

  • Save as you sign in: new logins can be saved straight from the sign-in screen you just used
  • Two-step sign-ins: handles sites that ask for the username and the password on separate pages
  • Never silent: you unlock and tap the suggestion; nothing is filled behind your back

Unlock with your fingerprint

Open a vault with your fingerprint or your phone's screen lock instead of typing the master password every time. It is off until you turn it on, separately for each vault, and it is protected by Android's hardware-backed Keystore.

The vault locks the moment you leave the app, so a quick unlock matters.

Sync through your own cloud drive

Keep the vault file in Google Drive or another storage app on your phone to use it on more than one device. Native Creds has no cloud of its own; the storage app moves the encrypted file.

When the file was changed on another device, Native Creds merges the changes instead of overwriting them, so edits made on two phones are both kept. Sync now and pull-to-refresh fetch the latest copy on demand.

If two phones edit the very same entry, one whole version wins and the other is kept in the entry's history.

Move in from your browser or another manager

Import passwords from Google Password Manager and Chrome, and from CSV exports of Edge, Firefox, Safari and Apple Passwords, Bitwarden, LastPass, Dashlane, NordPass and Proton Pass.

You review duplicates before anything is added, and Native Creds helps you delete the exported file afterwards.

Passkeys cannot be imported, and 1Password's own export format is not supported yet.

Strong passwords, made on your phone

The built-in generator makes random passwords with the length and character types you choose, right where you are editing an entry. Nothing about them is sent anywhere; there is nowhere to send it.

Open a vault file from anywhere

Tap a .kdbx file in your file manager, Drive or a mail app and choose Native Creds. It checks what the other app allows and tells you plainly: a normal vault, a vault you can change only until you close it, or a read-only one, with Save a copy to get one you can edit.

Light, dark, or follow your phone

A calm porcelain-and-blue light theme and a true-black dark theme that follows your phone's setting, or stays put if you choose. Change it any time in Settings.

Careful by default

It locks when you leave

Press home, switch apps or turn the screen off and the vault locks straight away. There is no timer to forget about.

The clipboard cleans up

Copied passwords and codes are marked sensitive and cleared after 30 seconds, even after you switch to another app.

Screens stay private

Screenshots, screen recording and the recent-apps preview are blocked. Native Creds' own data is left out of Android backups.

What it does not do yet

Native Creds is an independent app made by one developer, and it is honest about its limits:

  • Passkeys are not supported yet (planned for after launch, no date promised).
  • No QR scanning for two-factor setup, and no master-password change inside the app.
  • Android only for now: no iPhone, Mac, Windows or browser extension version.

The full list is in the FAQs.
